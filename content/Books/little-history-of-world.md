Inward thinkers like Buddaa and Lao Tzu say when looking inward you should not care about anything or want anything. Whereas outward thinkers like Kung Fu Tzu say that when looking outward you should care about others like family, friends, and community.

### CHAPTER 10
#### THE ENLIGHTENED ONE AND HIS LAND

Buddha

> if we want to avoid suffering, we must start with ourselves, because all suffering comes from our own desires. Think of it like this. If you are sad because you can’t have something you want – maybe a book or a toy – you can do one of two things: you can do your best to get it, or you can stop wanting it. Either way, if you succeed, you won’t be sad any more.

### CHAPTER 11
#### THE GREAT TEACHER OF A GREAT PEOPLE

Confucius or Kung Fu Tzu

> outward appearances are more important than we think – bowing to our elders, letting others go through a door first, standing up to speak to a superior, and many other similar things for which they had more rules in China than we have

Lao Tzu

> do nothing. Be still within ourselves. Neither look nor listen to anything around us, have no wishes or opinions. Only when a person has become like a tree or a flower, empty of all will or purpose, will he begin to feel the Tao – that great universal law which makes the heavens turn and brings the spring – begin to work within him
